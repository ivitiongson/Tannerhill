You asked me to write down how a message actually travels from a customer to an answer. Here it is. It is not a process anybody designed, it is what happened while we were busy.

---

## Where messages arrive

Three places, and they do not talk to each other.

**Amazon buyer messages.** Inside Seller Central. Only Priya and I have logins. Amazon starts a clock and dings our metrics if we are slow, so these get looked at first even when something else is more urgent.

**The contact form on our site.** Lands in a shared Gmail inbox, `hello@`. Lena, Priya and I are all in there.

**Kickstarter comments and backer messages.** On the campaign page. Nobody checks these on a schedule. Priya looks when she remembers.

There is no ticketing system. There is no queue. There is no single place where you can see everything waiting.

---

## Who picks it up

Whoever sees it first. That is the honest answer.

What tends to happen in practice:

- I do the shared inbox most mornings, usually around 08:30, and again if I remember after lunch
- Priya handles anything that is pre-sale, product specifications, or Kickstarter
- Anything about a warranty, a refund, or an angry customer ends up with Lena

That last one is not a rule anybody wrote. It is just that Priya sends warranty questions to me because she does not know the answer, and I send them to Lena because I do not either.

Nothing marks a message as taken. Twice this month two of us have replied to the same person.

---

## What a person does with one message

Roughly this, every time:

1. Read it and work out what they actually want
2. Find the order. Shopify admin if it came through the site, Seller Central if it came through Amazon
3. If it is a status question, open the tracking link and read it
4. If it is a product question, check the product page or ask Priya
5. If it is about a warranty or a return, stop and ask somebody
6. Write a reply
7. Send it

Step 5 is where things sit for days.

---

## Where the answers come from

| What is being asked | Where the answer lives |
| --- | --- |
| Where is my order | Shopify admin, or Seller Central, plus the carrier tracking link |
| Has it shipped | Same |
| Product specifications, compatibility, care | The product page, or Priya |
| Delivery estimates | Nowhere reliable. The site says one to two business days and that has not been true since January. |
| Warranty coverage | Three documents that disagree, plus whatever Lena says |
| Returns | Same problem |
| Anything about money | Lena only |

---

## What gets written down

Nothing.

There is no log of what was asked, what we decided, or who decided it. If a customer comes back a month later we search the inbox and hope. If somebody asks me how many warranty claims we approved last quarter I cannot tell them.

The response times in the pack I sent you, I had to read every message and work them out by hand. That is the only reason those numbers exist.

---

## Where a message ends up

Four endings, and only two of them are good.

- **Answered.** The customer gets a reply and that is the end of it.
- **Handed to somebody else.** Priya to me, me to Lena. No record of the handoff. It lives in whoever's head until they deal with it.
- **Waiting on a decision.** Mostly warranty and returns. These are the ones that sit.
- **Forgotten.** It happens. Five messages in the pack I sent never got a reply at all, and nobody noticed until I counted.

---

## What I would tell you if you asked me what is broken

The sorting is the work, not the answering. Working out which ones I can deal with and which ones I have to pass on is what eats the morning.

To be fair to it, most of this is easy. Nine out of ten I could answer in under a minute if I knew the rule, and a lot of them are the same three questions over and over. It is a small number of messages causing nearly all of the trouble, and they are the warranty and returns ones, because three of us touch those before the customer hears anything.
